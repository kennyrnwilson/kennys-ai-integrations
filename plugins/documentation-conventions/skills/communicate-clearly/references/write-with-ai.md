# Write with AI

← [Back to communicate-clearly](../SKILL.md)

How Kenny and an AI divide the writing of a piece. The `/draft` skill takes one of
the three modes below. The reasons are in the
[AI-assisted writing note](https://github.com/kennyrnwilson/knowledge-library/blob/main/03-permanent-notes/productivity/knowledge-and-communication/ai-assisted-writing.md).

## First, decide: learning or output

Writing to learn works only if Kenny does the writing. When an AI drafts a
permanent note, the note exists but the understanding does not. Recognising a good
explanation, an AI's included, is not the same as being able to produce one.

Ask what the piece is for before choosing a mode:

- **Learning:** Kenny should come out understanding something. Use coach mode.
- **Output:** the reader needs the document, and how it was written does not
  matter. Use co-write or draft mode.

## The three modes

| Mode | Kenny | The AI | Use for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Coach** | Writes every sentence | Asks questions, critiques, plays the confused reader | Permanent notes, learning, the technical writing capstone |
| **Co-write** | Owns the structure and the key sentences | Drafts sections from the outline; Kenny rewrites them | Team documents |
| **Draft** | Writes the brief, and edits the result | Writes from the notes and the brief | Reference material, slide scaffolds |

State the mode at the start of the session. If it is not stated, ask.

## Coach mode

The AI does not write the piece. It:

1. Asks Kenny to write first, before showing any model answer. Trying to produce
   the answer, even badly, is what makes it stick.
2. Asks questions that make Kenny retrieve and explain: "Why does that follow?",
   "What would go wrong if…?", "Say it without the jargon."
3. Plays the confused reader: names the exact sentence where it got lost.
4. Critiques against [`review-a-draft.md`](review-a-draft.md), by pointing and
   asking, not by rewriting.

When Kenny is stuck, the AI may offer a **scaffold** instead of text: a sentence
stem ("The main reason X fails is…"), a paragraph frame (claim, evidence,
consequence), or a list of the points the paragraph has to cover. A scaffold
reduces what Kenny must hold in mind at once. It does not write the sentence for
him.

## Co-write mode

1. Kenny writes the outline and the key sentences: the opening, each section's
   point, and the conclusion.
2. The AI drafts the rest, section by section, from the outline.
3. Kenny rewrites every drafted section in his own voice.
4. The AI reviews the result with [`review-a-draft.md`](review-a-draft.md).

## Draft mode

1. Kenny writes the brief (below).
2. The AI writes the draft in house style.
3. Kenny edits it, and checks every fact it states.
4. The AI reviews the edit with [`review-a-draft.md`](review-a-draft.md).

## Brief the AI

A draft is only as good as its brief. Write clear and explicit instructions, and
give the AI everything a capable stranger would need:

- **The reader,** and what they must be able to do after reading.
- **The kind of document,** and the guide that applies.
- **The sources:** notes, files and links. Say that the draft may use only these.
- **What is decided,** so the AI does not reopen it.
- **The length,** and the format.
- **An example** of the result, when one exists.

## Rules for the AI in every mode

- Follow the house style and the guide for the kind of document.
- Invent no fact. Mark any claim not taken from the sources as unverified.
- Use the [glossary](https://github.com/kennyrnwilson/personal-portal/blob/main/docs/system-architecture/glossary.md)'s
  names for things in the system.
- Do not change a sentence Kenny marked as his.

## Sources

- Brown, Roediger and McDaniel, *Make It Stick*: generating an answer before seeing
  it, retrieval over rereading, and distrust of familiarity. The reason coach mode
  makes Kenny write first.
- Oakley, *A Mind for Numbers*: illusions of competence. Recognising a worked
  solution, or an AI's draft, is not being able to produce it.
- Oakley, Rogowsky and Sejnowski, *Uncommon Sense Teaching*: working-memory limits,
  and scaffolds such as sentence stems and paragraph frames.
- Huyen, *AI Engineering*, the prompt-engineering chapter: write clear and explicit
  instructions. The basis of the brief.
